THE NEUROSCIENCE
What's Actually Happening When Your Child Screams
The Neuroscience
The scalp is one of the most nerve-dense areas of the human body — approximately 100,000 hair follicles, each surrounded by sensory nerve endings connected to the somatosensory cortex.
In typically developing children, the brain filters and modulates these signals — brushing registers as "touch" not "threat."
In children with tactile over-responsivity, the somatosensory cortex over-amplifies light touch signals. Brushing registers on pain pathways rather than simple touch pathways.
This is a wiring difference, not a behavior choice.
In Plain English
Your child's brain has the volume turned up to maximum on touch signals from the scalp. What feels like gentle brushing to you feels like scratching sandpaper across sunburned skin to them.

MATERIAL 1 OF 9
Scalp Massager
Spider-Leg Handheld Style · ₹150–400 · Category: Preparation
Purpose
Prepares the nervous system through deep pressure input BEFORE any brushing begins. Activates the proprioceptive system, which is calming and organizing. Tells the brain: "Touch on the head is safe."
DIY Alternative
Firm fingertip circles across entire scalp, 2–3 minutes. Slow, circular, deep pressure. Works identically. Cost: ₹0.

Safety Note: Never use on open wounds or scalp infections. Start on arms/shoulders first if child resists head touch.
