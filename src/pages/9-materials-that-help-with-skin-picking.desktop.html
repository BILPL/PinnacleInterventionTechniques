Sensory Redirection for Body-Focused Repetitive Behaviors (BFRB)
Give the Fingers Somewhere Else to Go
Skin picking (excoriation disorder / dermatillomania) is a Body-Focused Repetitive Behavior (BFRB) in which the child repeatedly picks at their own skin — often at scabs, bumps, blemishes, or imperfections — resulting in wounds that don't heal, scarring, and significant emotional distress. The behavior typically occurs outside conscious awareness and serves a regulatory function: it provides sensory input, reduces anxiety, induces a focused state, or manages emotional overwhelm. It is not a choice, not attention-seeking, and not a discipline problem.
Sensory Redirection is the evidence-based approach of replacing the picking behavior with materials that meet the same underlying need — giving the nervous system what it's searching for, through a safe and constructive channel. The 9 materials on this page represent the full spectrum of BFRB functions: tactile seeking, peeling, tension discharge, barriers, anxiety relief, awareness, skin healing, hand occupation, and competing responses.

This Technique Crosses Every Therapy Boundary
Occupational Therapy (OT) — Primary Lead
Conducts sensory processing evaluation to identify the specific function of picking. Selects and prescribes materials matched to the child's sensory profile. Teaches proper implementation and builds home toolkit.
ABA / BCBA — Behavioral Architecture
Conducts functional behavior assessment (FBA) to identify antecedents, functions, and consequences. Designs competing response protocols, reinforcement schedules, and data collection systems. Integrates with ComB framework.
NeuroDevelopmental Pediatrics — Medical Oversight
Rules out dermatological causes, assesses for co-occurring anxiety/OCD requiring medication, monitors wound infections, and coordinates multi-disciplinary approach. Provides diagnosis of excoriation disorder when warranted.
Special Education (SpEd) — School Integration
Ensures picking management strategies transfer to school settings. Provides classroom fidget access, communicates with teachers, and builds IEP accommodations. Eliminates shame-based school responses.
